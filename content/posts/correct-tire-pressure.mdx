People check their tire pressure, see "MAX PRESS 51 PSI" on the sidewall, and fill to 51. That's the tire's safety ceiling, not a target. Running there makes the ride harsh, shrinks the tire's grip on the road, and wears the center of the tread out early.

---

## Where the right number actually is

Your car's manufacturer sets the correct pressure for your specific vehicle — its weight, its handling, its brakes. You'll find it on the **tire placard**, a sticker usually on the **driver's door jamb** (open the door and look at the edge of the frame). Sometimes it's in the glove box door or the fuel door, and it's always in the owner's manual.

It lists the recommended **cold** pressure for front and rear tires, which aren't always the same — and sometimes the spare too.

## Check it cold

"Cold" means the car has sat for a few hours, or been driven less than about a mile. Driving heats the air in the tires and raises the reading by a few PSI, so checking right after a highway drive makes an underinflated tire look fine.

**Don't let air out of hot tires** to match the placard — they'll be low once they cool.

## Why it matters

- **Underinflation** is the more common problem. It makes tires flex and heat up, wears the edges, hurts fuel economy, and makes a blowout more likely. Tires naturally lose roughly 1 PSI a month, even without a leak
- **Cold weather drops pressure** — roughly 1 PSI for every 10°F drop in temperature. The first cold morning of fall is when a lot of dashboard warning lights come on
- **Overinflation** means less rubber on the road, less grip (especially in rain), and a harsher ride

## The dashboard light isn't a substitute

Cars built since the 2008 model year have a tire pressure monitoring system (TPMS). It's a warning, not a gauge: it typically doesn't trigger until a tire is about **25% below** the recommended pressure. You can lose a lot of pressure before it lights up.

## How to actually check

1. Get a gauge — a basic one costs a few dollars, and gas station air machines are often inaccurate
2. Unscrew the valve cap, press the gauge on firmly, read it
3. Add air in short bursts and recheck until you hit the placard number
4. Do all four tires (and the spare, if you have one — a flat spare is a bad surprise)

Once a month and before long trips is the standard advice.

## Quick reference

- **Sidewall number = maximum, not the target**
- **Correct pressure = sticker on the driver's door jamb (or owner's manual)**
- **Check cold — before driving or after sitting a few hours**
- **Cold weather drops pressure about 1 PSI per 10°F**
- **The TPMS light only triggers when a tire is ~25% low — check monthly anyway**
