Data brokers are companies whose entire business is compiling personal information — your address history, relatives, phone numbers, income estimates, property records — and selling access to it. Most people have never interacted with them directly and don't know they exist until they search their own name and find a "people search" site listing their address.

---

## Where the data comes from

Data brokers pull from public records (property deeds, voter registration, court records), commercial sources (loyalty programs, warranty cards, purchase histories sold by retailers), and other data brokers who resell to each other. None of it required your direct consent in the way signing up for an account would — it's aggregated from sources that were already technically public or contractually shareable.

## It's legal, which is exactly the problem

There's no single federal law banning this. A patchwork of state privacy laws (California, and a growing number of other states) gives residents the right to request deletion from data brokers operating in that state, and some states now maintain broker registries or centralized opt-out mechanisms. Coverage and process still vary a lot depending on which state you live in and which broker you're dealing with — check your own state's current consumer privacy law for what you're actually entitled to.

## The manual process, since there's no single button that fixes everything

1. **Search your own name** on major people-search sites (Spokeo, Whitepages, BeenVerified, MyLife, and similar) to see what's actually out there
2. **Use each site's own opt-out page** — nearly all of them have one, usually buried in the footer under "privacy" or "do not sell my info," specifically because state laws require it
3. **Expect to repeat this periodically.** Brokers re-scrape public records and can re-list you months later, so a one-time opt-out isn't permanent for most people
4. **Opt out of pre-screened credit and insurance offers** separately at optoutprescreen.com or 1-888-5-OPT-OUT — this is a federally mandated, genuinely permanent (or 5-year, your choice) opt-out run jointly by the major credit bureaus, and it's different from broker opt-outs
5. **Register your phone number** at donotcall.gov to reduce telemarketing tied to sold contact info, understanding it won't stop scammers who ignore the registry anyway

## Paid services exist, and just automate the manual process

Services that charge a monthly or annual fee to "remove you from data broker sites" are generally doing the same opt-out requests you could do yourself, at scale, on a recurring schedule. That can be worth paying for if you don't want to repeat the manual process yourself every few months — it's not doing anything you fundamentally can't do on your own.

## Why this is worth doing at all

Data broker listings are a common ingredient in **doxxing, stalking, and targeted scams** — someone doesn't need to hack anything if your home address, relatives' names, and phone number are sitting on a $20 background-check site. This matters more for some people than others, but the baseline exposure is the same for almost everyone.

---

## Quick reference

- **Data brokers legally compile and sell your personal info from public and commercial sources**
- **State laws (not one federal law) govern your opt-out rights, and vary by where you live**
- **Opt out site-by-site through each broker's own footer link — there's no universal off switch yet**
- **optoutprescreen.com is a separate, federally mandated opt-out for pre-screened credit offers**
- **Expect to redo opt-outs periodically — brokers re-scrape and re-list over time**
