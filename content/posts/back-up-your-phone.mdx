Phones get dropped in water, stolen, or just stop turning on. The phone itself is replaceable. What's on it — years of photos, your contacts, and the codes that prove you're you — often isn't, unless it's backed up.

---

## iPhone

- **iCloud Backup:** Settings → your name → iCloud → iCloud Backup → turn it on. It backs up automatically when your phone is charging, locked, and on Wi-Fi
- **Storage:** Apple includes 5 GB free, which fills up fast once photos are involved. Check Settings → your name → iCloud to see what's using space. The cheapest paid tier is inexpensive and usually enough for one person
- **Or back up to a computer:** plug into a Mac (Finder) or a Windows PC (the Apple Devices app, or iTunes on older setups). Choose "Encrypt local backup" so passwords and health data are included

## Android

- **Google backup:** Settings → Google → Backup (the exact path varies by phone brand) → turn on Backup by Google One. It covers app data, call history, contacts, settings, and SMS
- **Photos:** Google Photos backs up photos and videos separately — open the app and make sure backup is on
- **Storage:** Google includes 15 GB free, shared across Gmail, Drive, and Photos

## The step almost everyone forgets: your two-factor codes

If you use an authenticator app for two-factor logins and your phone dies, you can be locked out of your own accounts. Before that happens:

- **Turn on your authenticator's backup or sync** if it has one (Google Authenticator and Microsoft Authenticator both can sync to your account; many password managers store codes too)
- **Save your backup/recovery codes** when you set up 2FA on important accounts — print them or store them in your password manager
- **Passkeys** saved in iCloud Keychain or Google Password Manager sync to a new phone automatically

## The 3-2-1 rule, simplified

The rule IT people use: **3 copies** of anything important, on **2 different kinds of storage**, with **1 copy somewhere else**. For most people that means: the phone itself, a cloud backup, and an occasional copy of your photos to a computer or external drive.

## Check that it's actually working

A backup you've never checked is a guess. Once in a while, look at the "last backup" date (it's shown on the same settings screen). If it says weeks ago, something — usually full storage — is stopping it.

## Quick reference

- **iPhone: Settings → your name → iCloud → iCloud Backup**
- **Android: Settings → Google → Backup, plus backup on in Google Photos**
- **Back up your authenticator app and save 2FA recovery codes**
- **3-2-1: three copies, two kinds of storage, one somewhere else**
- **Check the "last backup" date every so often**
