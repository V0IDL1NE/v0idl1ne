Robocalls and mailers warning that your car's warranty is "about to expire" are consistently among the most reported scam and spam categories tracked by federal regulators. Almost none of them are from your manufacturer or dealer.

---

## They're not warranties — they're service contracts

What's being sold in these calls is technically a **vehicle service contract** (sometimes called a "mechanical breakdown insurance" product), not a warranty. Only the manufacturer or original dealer can extend an actual factory warranty. Everyone else selling you "extended warranty coverage" is selling a service contract from a third-party company — which can be legitimate, junk, or an outright scam, and the caller isn't going to tell you which.

## How they get your information

They don't have your VIN, your mileage, or your actual warranty status when they call — they're guessing based on the fact that you own a car at all, often from lists of registered vehicle owners or just random dialing. A real warranty-related notice from your manufacturer comes by mail from the manufacturer directly, references your specific VIN, and doesn't create urgency with a countdown.

## Red flags that mean it's not legitimate

- **Robocall or unsolicited mailer** creating urgency ("final notice," "expires soon")
- **They ask for payment immediately** over the phone before sending you anything in writing
- **Vague company name** that doesn't match any manufacturer or a recognizable insurer
- **Pressure to decide on the call** instead of being allowed to review paperwork first
- **They already "know" your car is old enough to need this** without asking basic details like mileage or VIN

## If you want an actual extended warranty

- Go through your **dealer or manufacturer directly** for a manufacturer-backed extended warranty
- If considering a third-party service contract, verify the company's licensing with your state's department of insurance — many of these contracts are legally regulated as insurance products
- Read the contract for what's actually excluded — many cheap service contracts exclude the exact expensive repairs (transmission, engine internals) that would make the contract worth having
- Check reviews and complaint history with your state attorney general's office or the Better Business Bureau before paying anything

## What to do with the calls

Don't engage, don't press any button "to be removed" (that often just confirms your number is active), and hang up. You can report unwanted robocalls to the FTC at reportfraud.ftc.gov and register your number at donotcall.gov — it won't stop scam calls specifically, since scammers ignore the registry, but it does create a paper trail and reduces legitimate telemarketing.

## Quick reference

- **"Your car warranty is expiring" calls are selling service contracts, not real warranties**
- **They don't actually know your VIN or warranty status — they're guessing**
- **Urgency, upfront payment demands, and vague company names are the tell**
- **Get real extended coverage through your dealer/manufacturer or a verified, licensed provider**
- **Report robocalls to reportfraud.ftc.gov; don't press any callback or "removal" button**
