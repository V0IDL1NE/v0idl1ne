That little horseshoe-shaped light with an exclamation point in it isn't a nice-to-have feature. It's required by federal law, and the law exists because of one of the deadliest product scandals in American history.

---

## Tires coming apart

In the late 1990s, a pattern started showing up in crash reports. **Ford Explorers**, then one of the best-selling vehicles in the country, were rolling over after their **Firestone** tires failed at highway speed. The tread was **peeling away from the body of the tire** — a failure called tread separation — often in hot climates and at high speeds.

The crashes were brutal because an SUV losing a tire at 70 mph can easily roll. At least **238 people were killed and around 500 injured** in the U.S., with more deaths in other countries.

## The recall

In **August 2000**, Firestone recalled about **6.5 million tires.** Then the two companies turned on each other in public.

A big part of the fight was about **tire pressure.** Ford had recommended inflating the Explorer's tires to **26 psi**, on the low side. Firestone argued it should have been **30 psi**, and that underinflation made the tires run hotter and fail. Ford blamed the tires. Within a year, the nearly century-old business relationship between the two companies was over.

## The law

Congress held hearings and passed the **TREAD Act**, signed in November 2000. Among other things, it required carmakers to report problems to federal regulators faster — and it led to a requirement that new cars come with a **tire pressure monitoring system (TPMS).**

The rule phased in starting in 2005, and since **September 2007**, every new light vehicle sold in the U.S. has had to have one.

## The catch most people don't know

The federal rule only requires the light to come on when a tire is **25% or more below** the recommended pressure. On a tire that should be at 35 psi, that's about 26 psi. By the time the light comes on, the tire is already **seriously low** — not just a little off.

## What this means for you

- **Don't wait for the light.** Check your pressure about once a month, with the tires cold.
- **Use the number on the driver's door sticker**, not the one on the tire. [Why the tire's number is the wrong one.](/blog/correct-tire-pressure)
- **If the light comes on, check all four tires soon.** If it flashes and then stays on, the sensor system itself may have a problem.
- **Check your tread too.** [The penny test is later than you think.](/blog/tire-tread-penny-test)
