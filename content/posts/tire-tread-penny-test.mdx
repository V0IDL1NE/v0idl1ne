The penny test has been repeated for decades: stick a penny into your tire tread, Lincoln's head down. If you can see the top of his head, your tread is worn out. It's not wrong — it's just late.

---

## What the penny test actually measures

Inserting a penny with Lincoln's head down and seeing the top of his head means your tread has worn down to about **2/32 of an inch** — which is the legal minimum tread depth in most states. That's the point tires are considered legally worn out and unsafe, not the point to start paying attention.

## The quarter test catches it earlier

Use a quarter instead, with Washington's head down. If you can see the top of his head, you're at about **4/32 of an inch** — the point where most tire experts and manufacturers recommend you start actively shopping for replacements, especially before wet or winter driving season. Wet-weather stopping distance and hydroplaning resistance both drop noticeably between 4/32" and 2/32", well before tires are "legally" bald.

## The built-in indicator that doesn't require a coin at all

Every modern tire has **wear bars** molded into the tread grooves — small raised rubber bridges running perpendicular to the tread pattern. When the surrounding tread wears down to be flush with these bars, the tire is at the 2/32" legal limit. Look for small triangle markers on the tire's sidewall (▲) — they point to where the wear bars are located in the tread.

## Why tread depth matters more than people think

- **Wet traction** drops sharply as tread shallows, since there's less channel depth to move water out from under the tire — this is the main mechanism behind hydroplaning
- **Snow and ice traction** depends heavily on tread depth and pattern; worn tires lose most of their winter capability well before summer capability
- **Stopping distance** on a worn tire can be dramatically longer in wet conditions than on a tire with healthy tread, even if the tire looks "fine" walking past it

## Check more than one spot

Tires wear unevenly. Check tread depth at the outer edge, center, and inner edge of each tire — uneven wear patterns (more worn on one edge) are also a sign of alignment or inflation problems worth getting checked, separate from overall tread depth.

## Quick reference

- **Penny test (2/32") = legally worn out, already too late to be optimal**
- **Quarter test (4/32") = time to start shopping for new tires**
- **Wear bars molded into the tread do the same job without a coin**
- **Wet and winter traction degrade well before tires look "bald"**
- **Check tread at multiple points across each tire, not just one spot**
