Errors on credit reports are common: accounts that aren't yours, late payments you made on time, debts listed twice, balances that were paid off years ago. They can cost you a loan, an apartment, or a better interest rate. Fixing them is free, and the law is on your side.

---

## Step 1: Get all three reports

Go to **AnnualCreditReport.com** — the only official, federally authorized site. You can get free reports from **Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion every week**, permanently. Pull all three: lenders don't all report to every bureau, so an error can show up on one and not the others.

Ignore sites that advertise "free credit reports" and then ask for a card number.

## Step 2: Look for the usual errors

- **Accounts you don't recognize** — could be a mix-up with someone with a similar name, or identity theft
- **Payments marked late that weren't**
- **The same debt listed twice** — often once by the original creditor and once by a collector, both showing a balance
- **Wrong balances or credit limits** (a wrong limit can make your utilization look worse)
- **Closed accounts shown as open**, or accounts you closed shown as "closed by creditor"
- **Negative items older than about 7 years**, which generally have to come off
- **Your personal info** — wrong addresses or name variations can be a sign of mixed files

## Step 3: Dispute with the bureau

Dispute with **each bureau that shows the error**, on its website, by mail, or by phone. Online is fastest; mail (certified, with return receipt) creates the best paper trail.

Include:
- Your name, address, and the report's file or confirmation number
- Each item you're disputing and **exactly why it's wrong**
- Copies (never originals) of proof: bank statements, payment confirmations, a letter showing the account was paid or closed, a police or FTC identity theft report

## Step 4: The 30-day clock

Under the Fair Credit Reporting Act, the bureau generally has **30 days** to investigate (up to 45 if you send more information during that window). It must forward your dispute to the company that reported the item, and then:

- If the item is wrong, or can't be verified, it has to be **corrected or deleted**
- The bureau must send you the **results in writing**, and a free updated report if anything changed

You can also dispute directly with the company that reported it (the "furnisher") — it has the same duty to investigate.

## If the dispute fails

- **Ask what they checked.** You're entitled to a description of how they verified it
- **Add a statement** (up to 100 words) to your file explaining your side
- **File a complaint with the CFPB** at consumerfinance.gov/complaint — companies respond to these
- For serious, persistent errors, a consumer attorney can help; the FCRA lets you recover damages and attorney's fees if a bureau or furnisher breaks the law

## About "credit repair" companies

They can't legally remove accurate negative information, and they can't charge you before they've done the work. Anything they *can* do, you can do yourself for free with the steps above.
