Freezing your credit at all three bureaus costs nothing and takes about fifteen minutes. Until 2018, it could cost money in a lot of states — every time you froze or unfroze. Here's what changed it.

---

## The hole

In **March 2017**, a fix was released for a serious security flaw in **Apache Struts**, software used to run websites. Companies running it were told to patch.

**Equifax** — one of the three big credit bureaus that keep files on nearly every adult in America — didn't patch its online credit dispute system. In **mid-May 2017**, more than two months after the fix came out, attackers got in through that exact hole.

## 76 days

The attackers stayed inside Equifax's systems, undetected, for about **76 days**, pulling out data. Equifax noticed at the end of July. It didn't tell the public until **September 7, 2017.**

What was taken: names, **Social Security numbers, birth dates, addresses**, some driver's license numbers, and around 209,000 credit card numbers. The records of about **147 million Americans** — roughly half the country — were exposed.

In between discovering the breach and announcing it, three Equifax executives sold almost **$1.8 million** of company stock.

## The fallout

- In 2019, Equifax agreed to a settlement of at least **$575 million** with the FTC, the CFPB, and the states.
- In 2020, the U.S. Justice Department charged **four members of China's military** with carrying out the hack.

## The law

Here's the part that matters to you. People were told the best protection was a **credit freeze** — which blocks lenders from pulling your file, so nobody can open new credit in your name. But in many states, the bureaus could charge a fee to freeze, and another fee to unfreeze.

The backlash helped push Congress to change that. Under a law signed in May 2018, starting **September 21, 2018**:

- **Credit freezes are free** at all three bureaus, to freeze and to unfreeze
- **Freezes requested online or by phone must be placed within one business day**, and lifted within one hour
- **Parents can freeze the credit of children under 16** for free
- **Fraud alerts last one year** instead of 90 days

## What this means for you

Your data was very likely in this breach, or one of the many since. A freeze is the single strongest thing you can do, and it's free.

- **Freeze all three: Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion.** Each one is separate.
- **Use the bureaus' official sites**, and save the PIN or login each gives you.
- **Unfreeze temporarily** when you apply for a loan or card, then it refreezes.
- **Don't pay for a "credit lock."** [Here's the difference.](/blog/credit-freeze-vs-lock)
