Water expands when it freezes. In a pipe, that builds pressure between the ice and a closed faucet until something gives — often a split that doesn't start spraying until the ice melts. A single burst pipe can release hundreds of gallons, so a few minutes of prevention on the coldest nights is worth it.

---

## Which pipes freeze

The ones exposed to cold air: pipes in **exterior walls**, **unheated garages, crawl spaces, basements, and attics**, under **kitchen and bathroom sinks on outside walls**, and outdoor hose bibs and sprinkler lines.

## Before a hard freeze

- **Disconnect garden hoses** and shut off and drain outdoor faucets, if you have an indoor shutoff valve for them
- **Insulate exposed pipes** in unheated areas with foam pipe sleeves — cheap and easy
- **Seal drafts** near pipes (gaps where pipes enter the house, around vents)
- **Know where your main water shutoff is** — see [find every water shutoff in your home](/blog/water-shutoff-locations)

## On the coldest nights

- **Let faucets on outside walls drip** — both hot and cold for single-handle faucets. Moving water is harder to freeze, and an open faucet relieves pressure if ice does form
- **Open cabinet doors** under sinks so the room's warm air reaches the pipes
- **Keep the thermostat steady**, day and night — don't turn it way down to save money on a bitter night
- **Going away? Leave the heat on.** The Red Cross says no lower than 55°F; insurance industry guidance says 65°F, because the inside of an exterior wall runs colder than the room. In a deep freeze, lean toward the warmer number
- **Keep the garage door closed** if there are water lines in the garage

## If a pipe is frozen

Signs: you turn on a faucet and get **only a trickle or nothing**, or you see frost on a pipe.

1. **Open the faucet** the pipe feeds. As ice melts, flowing water helps melt the rest, and it relieves pressure
2. **Apply gentle heat to the frozen section**, starting closest to the faucet and working back: a **hair dryer**, a **heating pad**, a **space heater kept a safe distance away** (never left unattended), or **towels soaked in hot water**
3. **Keep going until full water pressure returns**, then check other faucets — if one pipe froze, others may have too

**Never use a torch, propane heater, charcoal, or any open flame** — it's a fire risk, and overheating a pipe can make it burst. If you can't find or reach the frozen spot, or it won't thaw, call a plumber.

## If a pipe bursts

1. **Shut off the main water valve** immediately
2. **Turn off electricity** to the affected area if water is near outlets, appliances, or the panel — only if you can do it safely
3. **Photograph the damage** before cleanup, for insurance
4. **Call a plumber**, then your insurer (or your landlord, if you rent — it's generally their repair)
5. Start drying things out quickly — mold can start growing within a day or two

---

## Quick reference

- **Drip faucets on outside walls; open sink cabinets on cold nights**
- **Away from home? Leave the heat on — at least 55°F, and closer to 65°F in extreme cold**
- **Disconnect hoses and insulate exposed pipes before winter**
- **Frozen: open the faucet, warm the pipe gently — hair dryer, heating pad, warm towels**
- **Never use an open flame**
- **Burst: shut the main valve first, then call a plumber**
