Insurance covers what you can document. After a fire, flood, or theft, insurers don't take your word for what you owned — they want proof: photos, receipts, serial numbers, appraisals. Most people have none of that ready when they need it most.

---

## The fastest way to build one

Walk through every room with your phone recording video, narrating out loud as you go — open closets, drawers, cabinets. Say what things are and roughly what they cost as you film. This single video is worth more during a claim than most people's entire memory of what they owned.

Do this for:

- **Every room**, including closets, garages, and storage areas
- **High-value items specifically** — electronics, jewelry, tools, musical instruments, appliances — get a close-up on brand, model, and serial number where visible
- **Anything with an appraisal or original receipt** — keep those documents too, not just the video

## Store it somewhere the disaster can't reach

A home inventory saved only on a laptop that burns down with the house is useless. Back it up somewhere physically separate:

- **Cloud storage** (email it to yourself, upload to a cloud drive)
- **A copy with a family member or friend who lives elsewhere**
- Your insurance company or agent — some let you attach documentation to your policy file directly

## What it actually gets you

- **Faster claims processing** — adjusters move faster when documentation already exists instead of having to verify a written list from memory
- **Fewer disputes over value** — a video showing an item in use, with visible condition and detail, is stronger evidence than a claim form line item
- **Coverage for things you'd otherwise forget** — after a major loss, people reliably under-remember what they owned; a video doesn't have that problem

## Update it periodically

A home inventory from five years ago misses everything bought since. Redo the walkthrough:

- After any major purchase (electronics, furniture, appliances)
- Once a year as a baseline, even if nothing major changed
- Immediately after moving into a new place

This pairs directly with [renters insurance covers more than you think](/blog/renters-insurance) — the policy doesn't help if you can't document what it's supposed to be covering.

---

## Quick reference

- **Record a narrated video walkthrough of every room, including closets and storage**
- **Get close-ups of serial numbers and models on high-value items**
- **Store the video somewhere off-site — cloud storage or with someone else**
- **Redo it at least yearly, and right after big purchases**
- **Keep original receipts and appraisals alongside the video, not instead of it**
