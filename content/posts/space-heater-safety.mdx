Portable space heaters are consistently one of the leading causes of home heating fires in the US. The failure mode is almost always one of a handful of avoidable mistakes, repeated.

---

## The rules that actually matter

- **Three feet of clear space, on all sides.** Nothing flammable — curtains, bedding, furniture, paper, pet beds — within three feet of any surface of the heater.
- **Plug it directly into a wall outlet.** Never into an extension cord or a power strip. Space heaters typically draw close to 1,500 watts, which is already near the safe limit of a standard 15-amp circuit (see [amps, volts, watts](/blog/amperage-vs-wattage)) — a thin extension cord carrying that load is exactly the fire risk covered in [why your extension cord might be a fire hazard](/blog/extension-cord-gauge).
- **Never leave it running unattended**, and never run one overnight in a room while you're asleep unless it's specifically rated and designed for that use with tip-over and overheat shutoff.
- **Put it on a hard, level, non-flammable surface** — not carpet, not a rug, not a table edge where it could tip.

## Buy one with these two safety features

- **Automatic tip-over shutoff** — cuts power the instant the unit is knocked over
- **Overheat protection** — shuts the unit off if internal temperatures get dangerously high

Nearly every heater sold at a major retailer today has both, but older units — especially ones pulled out of storage from years ago — often don't. If a heater doesn't list these features, don't use it.

## Where not to use one

- **Bathrooms**, unless the specific unit is rated for damp locations — water and electricity plus an unattended heat source is a bad combination
- **Anywhere near water sources** in general
- **Small enclosed spaces without ventilation**, especially for combustion-based heaters (propane, kerosene) which can produce carbon monoxide — see [where smoke and CO detectors are legally required](/blog/smoke-co-detector-placement)

## The math on why this matters

Space heaters are popular specifically because central heating is expensive to run for one room. But a $30 heater used carelessly for one winter is a worse financial and safety outcome than the heating bill it was meant to offset.

---

## Quick reference

- **Three feet of clearance from anything flammable, always**
- **Plug directly into the wall — never an extension cord or power strip**
- **Never leave it running unattended or overnight unless it's rated for that**
- **Buy one with tip-over and overheat shutoff — most new units have both**
- **Keep off carpet, out of bathrooms, and away from bedding**
